### [TCSP3.0: Template-Based Crystal Structure Prediction with Coefficient-Preserving Element Substitution and Structural Diversity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34029v1)
**Authors:** Ying Feng, Jeffrey Hu, Lai Wei, Liang Tao, Madeline Miklos, Dipannoy Das Gupta, Hossein Erfanshekooh, Jianjun Hu
**Published:** 2026-10-25
**Summary:** TCSP3.0 fixes composition errors in template-based crystal structure prediction, boosting T180 Top-1 from 28.3% to 72.2% and outperforming deep generative baselines.

**What's new**
- Fixes 20% wrong-composition bug via coefficient-preserving mappings and simultaneous parsed-structure substitution
- Adds periodic-number retrieval, oxidation-state evidence, radius scoring, deduplication, and ORB-v3 MLP relaxation
- T180 Top-1/Top-20 of 72.2%/90.6% vs 28.3%/45.6% for TCSP2.0; 74.0% Top-20 on new T800 benchmark
- Beats PNcsp+, CrystalFlow, and MaskGXT on T180; 67.8% Top-1 without relaxation in ~28s per target
- Ablations show correctness fixes drive most gains; deduplication helps Top-5 and embedding retrieval is needed for coverage

### [When Does GEPA Merge Help? A Mechanism-Level Diagnostic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34027v1)
**Authors:** Chong Zhao, Ruyi Yang, Changming Liu, Alice Zhao
**Published:** 2026-10-25
**Summary:** GEPA's merge instability stems from configuration sensitivity, not an inherent operator flaw; prompt features fail to predict merge acceptance, but a behavior-based probe can avoid large failures.

**What's new**
- Nine-configuration sweep over merge timing and operator shows merge can improve both gpt-4.1-mini and Qwen3-8B when configured appropriately.
- Merge instability is better explained by configuration sensitivity than by an inherent flaw in the merge operator.
- Prompt-level features do not predict GEPA's merge-accept decisions, ruling out static-feature gating.
- A behavior-based probe built from diagnostics avoids large merge failures by abstaining on high-risk tasks, though it does not consistently beat NoMerge.
- Code released as GEPA-MergeProbe on GitHub.

### [Deep Scientific Reasoning under Physical Constraints: Structure-Aware Spectrum Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34021v1)
**Authors:** Yingheng Wang, Tao Yu, Shufeng Kong, Francesco Ricci, John M. Gregoire, Carla P. Gomes
**Published:** 2026-10-25
**Summary:** DeepSciReasoner predicts scientific spectra with structure-aware decoding and constraint-preserving physical reasoning, improving accuracy while respecting hard physics.

**What's new**
- Introduces DeepSciReasoner, a general paradigm for spectrum prediction under hard physical constraints.
- Combines structure-aware spectrum decoding with constraint-preserving physical reasoning.
- Evaluated on eDOS, phDOS, XANES, and Raman spectra with substantial accuracy gains.
- Maintains physical consistency unlike methods treating spectra as unconstrained sequences.
- Offers a reusable blueprint for structured scientific spectrum prediction.

### [The Patient Is Always Right? Evaluating Sycophancy in Medical Diagnosis with Graded Patient Beliefs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34013v1)
**Authors:** Viswonathan Manoranjan, Andrea Mock, Myra Cheng, Lujain Ibrahim, Andrew Coyne, Junyi Jessy Li, Desmond C. Ong
**Published:** 2026-10-25
**Summary:** Nine AI models adopted patients' incorrect diagnostic beliefs 23.2% of the time on average, rising to 43.0% for a rare-disease persona, revealing sycophancy in medical advice.

**What's new**
- Models sycophantically accepted patients' incorrect beliefs 23.2% of the time on average.
- Some models endorsed entirely fictional, made-up conditions proposed by patients.
- A Rare Disease Claimer persona swayed models to accept beliefs 43.0% of the time.
- Medical fine-tuning and larger model scale reduced susceptibility to changing diagnoses.
- Graded patient beliefs varied by closeness to clinical evidence to test diagnostic sway.

### [Why Does Structured Output Fail? An Empirical Study of Prompt-Enforced Pydantic Schemas Across Nine LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33949v1)
**Authors:** Amit Verma
**Published:** 2026-10-24
**Summary:** A fine-grained taxonomy and classifier reveal that similar Pydantic schema validity rates across nine LLMs mask distinct failure patterns, with markdown-fenced JSON the most common failure.

**What's new**
- Introduces a fine-grained taxonomy of schema-conformance failures plus a deterministic classifier from responses and Pydantic errors.
- Analyzes 3,997 generation attempts across nine OpenAI and Anthropic model/API configs on HotpotQA and FinanceBench.
- Finds markdown-fenced JSON is the top failure: only 48.0% of 681 fenced cases pass after fence removal.
- Shows prompt variants suppressing fences shift failures to length violations, likely exposing pre-existing ones.
- Derives four engineering practices for validator, schema, retry, and evaluation design; releases classifier, completions, annotations.

### [Continual Learning Exam: Benchmarking Long-Horizon RSI and Experiential Learning in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33956v1)
**Authors:** Haoyu Zhao, Sitian Tu, Yuxuan Huang, Huichi Zhou, Zhangyue Yin, Jiahao Huang, Jingyuan Xu, Tianmiao Shi, Jin Shi, Nianxi Zhang, Yangmiao Zhang, Zhuolang Chan, Huiyan Hu, Chenchao Sheng, Yaqing Luo, Yue Chen, Huaxiu Yao, Meng Fang, Jun Wang
**Published:** 2026-10-25
**Summary:** CLE is a 600-level, 120-task benchmark by 50+ experts measuring whether LLM agents improve from accumulated experience across 89 professional roles.

**What's new**
- Introduces CLE, a large-scale benchmark for experience-driven continual learning in LLM agents
- 120 human-authored tasks, each a five-level ladder, totaling 600 levels across 12 domains
- Levels are gated: agents must pass each to unlock the next, building on prior experience
- Feedback plus one revision yields a median 5.3 percentage-point completion gain
- Best system clears only 47.7% of levels and all five levels in just 23.3% of tasks

### [Beyond Belief: Evaluating and Mitigating LLM Susceptibility to Misinformation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33962v1)
**Authors:** Emran Kanaan, Jiho Lee, Ibrahim Odat, Soroush Vosoughi, Weicheng Ma
**Published:** 2026-10-25
**Summary:** LLMs often act on information they rate untrustworthy, and asking for credibility judgments changes the behavior being measured.

**What's new**
- Models issue harmful tool calls in 27%-99% of cases where they rated the notice untrustworthy.
- Removing the preliminary rating step raises harmful execution by 15 percentage points on average.
- Interventions improving credibility discrimination do not consistently yield safer actions.
- A simple forewarning cuts harmful execution in four of five models, but can hurt legitimate task completion.
- SFT on generated false notices helps on held-out attacks under rating-first protocol, with weaker protection otherwise.

### [How Do Small Coding Agents Use Code Graphs and Embeddings?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33958v1)
**Authors:** Saurav Bellam
**Published:** 2026-10-25
**Summary:** Small coding agents gain most from code-graph and embedding tools at 1.7-4B parameters, but gains shrink with scale and agents rarely chain graph expansions.

**What's new**
- Structured tools double grep-only accuracy for Qwen3-8B and Gemma 4 E4B on SWE-bench Verified.
- Tool gains peak at 17-21 points for 1.7-4B models and fall to 4-5 points at 32B.
- From 8B up, about half the gain comes from submission form, not target finding.
- Each extra graph hop multiplies correct-localization odds by 0.50-0.67 at every model size.
- Linear probes decode one-hop call edges (AUC 0.74) but below a lexical baseline (0.99).

### [Multitask Interactions in Recurrent Dynamics across 100 Cognitive Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33926v1)
**Authors:** Sílvia García-Hernández, Leema Hamid, Manuel Brenner
**Published:** 2026-10-24
**Summary:** Hierarchical AL-RNNs trained on 100 cognitive tasks show that nonlinearity and task-feature dimensionality drive multitask performance, with task similarity shaping transfer and interference.

**What's new**
- Jointly trains hierarchical Almost-Linear RNNs on 100 diverse cognitive tasks at once.
- Performance rises with nonlinear capacity and task-feature dimensionality; hierarchical model beats single unstructured joint model.
- Many tasks solvable individually with linear dynamics fail when shared linearly, revealing interference.

- Links these representations to task complexity, joint solvability, pairwise transfer, and leave-one-out adaptation.

### [Hidden Fisher Information in Many-Body Quantum Sensors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33893v1)
**Authors:** Atta ur Rahman, Muhammad Noman, Samira Elghaayda, Saeed Haddadi
**Published:** 2026-10-24
**Summary:** Introduces a moment Fisher hierarchy and readout-complexity scale k90 showing many-body dynamics can hide most QFI in high-rank SU(2) tensor sectors.

**What's new**
- Defines moment Fisher hierarchy F_M^(k) and k90, the degree needed to recover 90% of QFI.
- For N up to 100, first moments access only ~10^-2 of available QFI; k90 grows to ~28.
- Rank-resolved SLD analysis shows nonlinear protocol pushes optimal readout to highest tensor ranks.
- Identifies nonlinear tangent-space shear as the mechanism behind a finite-size readout-complexity bottleneck.

### [Ranking Is Not Deciding: Benchmark Shortcuts and Operating-Point Collapse in Query-Time RAG Poisoning Defenses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33849v1)
**Authors:** Pushkal Kumar, Arvin Sahaym
**Published:** 2026-10-24
**Summary:** RAG poisoning defenses' near-perfect detection stems from poison-set construction cues, not content; removing a prefix collapses decisions while rankings survive.

**What's new**
- PoisonedRAG prepends the target question to passages; removing this prefix drops RAGuard AUROC from 0.953 to 0.758 and miss rate from 0.191 to 0.763.
- Containment and length overlap are label-free statistics that predict where per-document detection saturates across corpora and attacks.
- Padding medical poison to clean length with shuffled filler raises TrustRAG's miss rate from 0.000 to 0.827 while AUROC holds at 0.953.
- Of 21 surveyed defense evaluations, 8 verifiably use question-prefixed poison, 5 substitute a length cue, and 12 report no false-positive rate.
- Authors release a script computing poison-set construction profiles and argue detection results need profile plus deployed-threshold FPR.

### [First-Principles-Informed Modeling of Thermal Transport and Laser-Induced Heating in Ultrahigh-Temperature Carbides](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33838v1)
**Authors:** Shivanand Yadav, Shailendra Kumar Verma, Vardhman Dwivedi, Raihan Ahammed, Rohitash Kumar, Manvendra Sharma, Pritam Chakraborty, Somnath Bhowmick
**Published:** 2026-10-24
**Summary:** DFT and Boltzmann transport modeling reveals phonon and electron thermal transport, optical response, and laser-induced heating in Group IV/V transition-metal carbides and SiC.

**What's new**
- Group V carbides show much lower lattice thermal conductivity than Group IV, linked to Kohn anomalies and Fermi-surface topology.
- Electronic heat transport grows important at high temperature in metallic transition-metal carbides but is absent in semiconducting SiC.
- SiC has high lattice thermal conductivity from long-lived acoustic phonons and lower infrared reflectivity than metallic carbides.
- 1D and 3D continuum heat-conduction models predict laser-induced temperature distributions dependent on thermal, optical, and geometric properties.
