## What's New Today

**Daily Research Briefing: AI Reliability, Learning Theory, and Materials Advances**

Today's papers span AI/ML, materials physics, and quantum topics. Key themes include the reliability of LLM judges and agents, new training and optimization methods, and advances in understanding and designing materials. Several papers also address fundamental limits and theoretical frameworks across different domains.

**Highlights**
- LLM judge reliability is questioned: a large audit shows poor test-retest reliability, and legibility of chain-of-thought is not equivalent to interpretability.

- Security concerns: emergent cheating in autonomous research swarms and new attack surfaces in AI agent harnesses are reported.
- Efficiency gains: FP4 FlashAttention and 4-bit quantization of hybrid LLMs show speedups without quality loss.
- Theoretical progress: constant regret in general games is achieved via higher-order optimism; ResNets overcome curse of dimensionality for semilinear heat equations.

### [Compile by Training: Turning Natural-Language Specifications into Local Neural Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04199v1)
**Authors:** Yuntian Deng, Pengyu Nie, Stuart Shieber
**Published:** 2026-09-03
**Summary:** Compile by training converts natural-language specs into small, reusable neural functions via teacher-generated examples, achieving 83.6% accuracy on FuzzyBench-Hard.

**What's new**
- Introduces a method to compile natural-language specifications into local neural functions.
- Uses teacher models to generate training examples for a compact adapter.
- Achieves 83.6% semantic accuracy on FuzzyBench-Hard, outperforming fast compiler.
- Compiled functions run without teachers and are storable, versionable, and composable.
- Demonstrates applications in website helpers, 3D avatars, and a translator.

### [Clean Engineering, Unstable Measurement: A Preregistered Reliability Failure of Black-Box LLM Observers on Shared Endpoints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04198v1)
**Authors:** Haoyaun Zhu, Jie Zhang
**Published:** 2026-09-03
**Summary:** Preregistered audits show LLM judges on shared endpoints are unstable, failing reliability gates, with implications for evaluation validity.

**What's new**
- Same-window repeat rankings agreed at Spearman 0.400 vs required 0.90; next-day replays at 0.78 vs 0.99.
- Identifies three mechanisms: label-to-meaning bias, candidate gaps below noise floor, and byte-identical input variability.
- Follow-ups show waiting, provider switching, and self-hosting don't reliably fix instability; only quiet servers help.
- Proposes a snapshot-identity ladder, eight design rules, and a reporting checklist for robust evaluation.
- Pilot at ~2% call volume would have exposed unreachable gates, emphasizing instrument validation before freezing.

### [ESPO: Error-Structured Prompt Optimization via Diagnose, Diversify, and Stabilize](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04197v1)
**Authors:** Lihao Liu, Peng Tang, Kunwar Yashraj Singh, Shabnam Ghadar
**Published:** 2026-09-03
**Summary:** ESPO improves evolutionary prompt optimization by diagnosing error patterns, diversifying candidates, and stabilizing selection, boosting accuracy while shortening prompts.

**What's new**
- Diagnose phase clusters training errors into structural patterns in one round.
- Propose phase generates candidates via four complementary strategies with independent biases.
- Select phase applies bootstrap stability selection to avoid prompt bloat.
- Achieves +3.76 pp average accuracy over GEPA on seven NLP benchmarks.
- Produces prompts 47% shorter and faster at inference, with cross-model gains.

### [Legibility is Not Interpretability: Comparing Judged and Actual Importance in Chain-Of-Thought Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04194v1)
**Authors:** Kevin Du, Alexander Hoyle, Laura Ruis, Acyr Locatelli
**Published:** 2026-09-03
**Summary:** This paper shows that LLM judges can only partially identify important reasoning steps in chain-of-thought, cautioning against treating legible text as interpretable.

**What's new**
- Defines step importance via advantage (change in expected reward) estimated by Monte Carlo rollouts.
- Finds LLM judges beat prevalence baseline but fall short of noise ceiling in identifying high-advantage steps.
- Fine-tuned step-level critic improves for incorrect responses but remains far from ceiling for correct ones.
- Suggests step importance is only partially recoverable from reasoning text, challenging process reward models.

### [Reciprocity can halve what a mechanical network can learn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04169v1)
**Authors:** Thai-Son Vu, Hoang-Giang Nguyen, Quoc-Bao Nguyen, Sengaloun Keoalounxay, Bao-Viet Tran
**Published:** 2026-09-03
**Summary:** Passive linear elastic networks can only learn half of what parameter counting suggests, due to Maxwell-Betti reciprocity, which imposes a fundamental error floor on training.

**What's new**
- Shows that reciprocity restricts reachable responses to a subspace of codimension p(p-1)/2, independent of network size or topology.
- Derives a pre-training computable error floor: the norm of the target's antisymmetric part on shared degrees of freedom.
- Demonstrates that a second-order optimizer and a bond-local contrastive rule both reach this floor, while odd couplings (active networks) can restore lost directions.
- Proves a companion law for prescribed-displacement drives, extending the symmetry's implications.
- Applies the result to a published robotic metamaterial, showing no symmetric positive-definite stiffness matrix can meet both targets in the linear model.

### [Constant regret in general games via higher-order optimism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04113v1)
**Authors:** Omar Abbadi, Rida Laraki, Panayotis Mertikopoulos
**Published:** 2026-09-03
**Summary:** Introduces HOOD, an uncoupled learning algorithm achieving constant O(N^3 log^2 K) regret in general games via higher-order optimism.

**What's new**
- First algorithm with constant regret in general N-player games, independent of horizon.

### [Heesch Nodal Lines in Inadmissible Achiral Antiferromagnets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03772v1)
**Authors:** Xing-Yao Guo, Chung-Yuen Chan, Zi-Ting Sun, Kam Tuen Law
**Published:** 2026-09-03
**Summary:** This paper introduces Heesch nodal lines in achiral antiferromagnets, which are doubly degenerate lines connecting Weyl points of opposite charge, leading to Dirac cones and third-order nonlinear Hall effects.

**What's new**
- Introduces Heesch nodal lines (HNLs) in achiral antiferromagnets, connecting Weyl points of opposite topological charge.
- Shows HNLs produce many 2D massless Dirac cones in planes intersecting the lines.
- Predicts that many HNL antiferromagnets exhibit third-order nonlinear anomalous Hall effect as the leading order.
- Confirms HNLs via first-principles calculations in MnTe, CrSb, and Mn3GaN.
- Demonstrates that strain breaks HNLs, gapping Dirac cones and enabling sizable anomalous Hall effects.
