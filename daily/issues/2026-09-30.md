# Daily Issue (JST): 2026-09-30

## What's New Today

**AI Agents, RL Post-Training, and Physics-Informed ML Dominate Today's Papers**

Today's submissions reveal a strong push toward making AI agents more reliable and efficient, from on-policy distillation and reinforcement learning for LLMs to tool-use and memory systems. In parallel, materials and physics research leverages deep learning for GW quasiparticle Hamiltonians, molecular property prediction, and quantum reservoir computing. A recurring theme is bridging the gap between theoretical guarantees and practical deployment, with several papers addressing staleness, safety, and interpretability in large models.

**Highlights**
- TaskBridge repurposes pretrained tabular foundation models for anomaly detection via virtual tasks, avoiding costly TAD-specific pretraining.
- DeepH-GW predicts GW quasiparticle Hamiltonians directly from atomic structure, enabling linear-scaling excited-state electronic structure.
- On-policy distillation advances: SAKI, IPD, and PR-OPD improve teacher-student alignment and credit assignment for LLM agents.
- GRPO variants proliferate: CorrGRPO, STAR-GRPO, and ACPO tackle reward correlation, hacking, and advantage clipping.
- Physics-informed ML: hard vs. soft constraints for Clapeyron equation, and neural network wave functions for electronic spectra.
- Safety and robustness: CounterSteer defends against indirect prompt injection, while VStress audits verifier reliability.
- Efficient inference: ARC-KV compacts KV caches, QuantMLA quantizes MLA, and SEED accelerates decoding via self-speculation.

**Themes**

### [TaskBridge: Bridging Unsupervised Tabular Anomaly Detection and In-Context Learning via Virtual Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36968v1)

### [Cool the Sampler, Not the Learner: Sampling Temperature Moves the Staleness Cliff of Importance-Corrected GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36953v1)
**Authors:** Taiheng Pan
**Published:** 2026-09-29
**Summary:** Cooling the sampler to temperature 0.8 while keeping the learner at 1 stabilizes importance-corrected GRPO and extends the safe refresh interval, matching shorter-interval performance.

**What's new**
- Identifies a staleness cliff: importance-corrected GRPO degrades severely after 180 steps when refreshed every 192 updates.
- Proposes decoupled cooling: sample at temperature 0.8 while learner, reference, and importance weights stay at 1.
- Cooled runs are stable and match uncooled runs refreshed twice as often (0.857 final, 0.79 average).
- On 7B, cooling makes interval 144 survivable, with cooled runs ending at 92-93% vs 68-81% uncooled.
- Benefit has limits: at 3x safe interval or high mismatch, cooling only delays degradation; stronger cooling is not better.

### [A systematic comparison of hard- and soft-constrained physics-informed molecular machine learning with the Clapeyron equation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36947v1)
**Authors:** Jan Pavšek, Jan G. Rittig, Alexander Mitsos
**Published:** 2026-09-29
**Summary:** Hard- and soft-constrained physics-informed ML are compared on Clapeyron-constrained vapor-liquid equilibrium; ALM improves soft constraints, hard constraints achieve higher consistency.

**What's new**
- First systematic comparison of hard- and soft-constrained physics-informed ML on experimental VLE data.
- Augmented Lagrangian method improves soft-constraint satisfaction by factor 2 and cuts training epochs by 30%.
- Hard-constrained approach reaches 12 orders of magnitude better Clapeyron consistency than soft-constrained.
- Hard constraints require tailoring model architecture to the specific thermodynamic relation.
- Soft-constrained approach with ALM is always recommended over fixed penalty weighting.
