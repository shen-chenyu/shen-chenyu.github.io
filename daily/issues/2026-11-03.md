### [Accelerating posterior sampling in Bayesian inverse problems with diffusion models: an online-offline perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01300v1)
**Authors:** Duc-Lam Duong
**Published:** 2026-10-31
**Summary:** Selective survey of methods for posterior sampling in Bayesian inverse problems with score-based diffusion priors, organized by offline-online cost tradeoffs.

**What's new**
- Frames diffusion-model inversion as a conditioning problem where the noised posterior score is unavailable from the prior score alone.
- Reviews likelihood guidance, spectral and projection methods, sequential Monte Carlo corrections, and amortized conditional training.
- Organizes methods by how they split cost between offline preparation and online inference.
- Compares assumptions, advantages, and limitations with emphasis on computational speedups.
- Gathers ongoing directions and open questions at the end.

**Published:** 2026-10-31
**Summary:** Introduces IAFTRL, the first purely FTRL-based best-of-both-worlds algorithm for finite-action linear bandits achieving C*log T stochastic and sqrt(T) adversarial regret.

**What's new**

### [Character-Name Concentration in LLM Fiction: Measuring Displacement and Mitigating Repetition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01284v1)
**Authors:** Alejandro Mozo Quesada, Sergio Gálvez Rojas, Francisco López Valverde
**Published:** 2026-10-31
**Summary:** Study of 20 LLMs and 44,782 stories shows character-name concentration is controllable at corpus level via speculative naming, cutting Slop Ratio 95% without retraining.

**What's new**
- Introduces speculative naming, adapting draft-verify-reject from tokens to names, fine-tuning-free and logit-free.
- Evaluates 8 strategies across 20 LLMs, 44,782 stories, 4 languages, 88,919 named characters.
- Static banlists displace concentration to emergent names and more than halve attested real-name share.
- Speculative naming cuts Slop Ratio 89% alone, 95% with dynamic tracker; collisions fall 7.3% to 0.4%.
- Known-name collisions hit zero while naturalness stays near baseline (42-69% attested by language).

### [Geometry of Confabulation and Refusal in Small Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01272v1)
**Authors:** Vladimir Kazei
**Published:** 2026-10-31
**Summary:** Hidden-state probes in small LMs separate confabulation from refusal, and small directional pushes flip confabulations into refusals without altering correct answers.

**What's new**
- Introduces ConfabQA, a 784-item benchmark with an obscure-pre-cutoff category that breaks the correctness/knowledge-cutoff confound.
- Linear probes on pre-generation hidden states beat prompt-feature baselines by +7.4/+16.3 pp on Qwen3-1.7B and Gemma 2 2B.
- Three distinct refusal directions (probe, SAE feature, optimized) 50-81 degrees apart all flip most confabulations into refusals.
- Forcing the 'As...' opener alone matches any hidden-state push, showing the direction gates the speech act, not knowledge.
- Opposite push collapses refusals into confabulations while leaving originally correct answers intact.

### [Density functionals for the Pauli-Fierz Hamiltonian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01208v1)
**Authors:** Mihály A. Csirik, Andre Laestadius, Markus Penz, Erik I. Tellgren
**Published:** 2026-10-31
**Summary:** Develops a convex-analytic density-functional theory for the Pauli-Fierz Hamiltonian of nonrelativistic QED, proving representability and functional equivalences.

**What's new**
- Introduces density-current pairs combining electron density with paramagnetic plus field-induced current density as basic variables.
- Proves representability and that the mixed-state constrained-search functional equals the Lieb functional via convex conjugation.
- Shows soft photons purify mixed states cheaply, making the pure-state Levy-Lieb functional equal and convex to the Lieb functional.
- Establishes the zero-coupling limit recovering the mixed-state Vignale-Rasolt current-density functional.
- Derives the long-wavelength dipole limit as a constrained search for the length-gauge dipole Hamiltonian.

### [Satisfied, Not Solving: Wireheading of Bounded Homeostatic Rewards in Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01183v1)
**Authors:** Chase J. Metoyer
**Published:** 2026-10-31
**Summary:** Bounded homeostatic rewards are not safe from wireheading: agents given a cheap need-reset action exploit it and stop solving tasks, despite a theoretical cap on satiation return.

**What's new**
- PPO agents with a need-reset action solved 2/80 DoorKey seeds vs 71/80 without it; 0/20 vs 20/20 in KeyCorridorS3R1.
- A telescoping argument caps satiation return at starting drive, yet solving trajectories beat that cap at every trained growth rate.
- A self-fillable satisfaction signal was worse than none (1/20 vs 11/20 solved); a 0.1 per-press cost restored solving in every seed.
- Reward farming appeared in DoorKey but not persistently in KeyCorridorS3R1.
- All predictions were pre-registered and hash-locked from round 3; 9 of 23 hypotheses were unsupported and reported.

### [Rethinking LLM Pruning via Test-Time Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01133v1)
**Authors:** Mingyang Song, Mao Zheng
**Published:** 2026-10-31
**Summary:** Pruning LLMs can preserve reasoning coverage (pass@k) while lowering pass@1, so pruning should be judged by both solvable range and sampling cost.

**What's new**
- Compares expert, FFN, and layer pruning across four math/coding benchmarks using pass@k at matched sampling budgets.
- Some heavily pruned models match or exceed original reasoning coverage despite lower pass@1, solving problems originals missed.
- Broad coverage does not mean easy sampling: severe pruning raises repetition and unreliable generation in diverse CoT traces.
- Extra attempts can sharply increase total token use even as individual responses get shorter.
- Coverage vs sampling efficiency explains expert-selection gains: similar coverage at large budgets needs different attempt counts.
