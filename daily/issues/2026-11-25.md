## Featured Papers

### [Eliciting the Recall Capacity of Linear Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32969v1)
**Authors:** Johan Lindqvist, Antonio Orvieto
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** Linear attention models have large untapped recall capacity; targeted training interventions like in-context repetition and loss reweighting elicit it and narrow the gap to softmax attention.

**What's new**
- Recall scales with state size similarly across tested linear-attention architectures.
- Even a single layer at scale can retrieve thousands of associations over long distances.
- Standard LM training fails to teach linear models to copy from context, unlike softmax models.
- In-context sentence repetition and context-sensitive loss reweighting improve recall benchmarks.
- These training changes narrow the long-distance associative recall gap without altering architecture or budget.

### [Quantum-Inspired Tensor Train Spectral Solver for High-Resolution PDEs with Non-Periodic Boundary Conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32968v1)
**Authors:** Sascha H. Hauck, Nis-Luca van Hülst, Matthias Kabel, Dieter Jaksch, Nicolas R. Gauger
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** Introduces superfast cosine and sine transforms in tensor train format, extending TT spectral PDE solvers to Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions.

**What's new**
- Derives SFCT and SFST from a shared TT operator representation based on quantum-circuit DCT/DST constructions.
- Proves TT ranks bounded by 6r_S independent of discretization order; measured ranks plateau near 32 up to 2^26 grid points.
- Rayleigh-Benard convection: spectral pressure projection gives up to 7x speedup in Poisson solve vs preconditioned physical-space TT solver.
- 3D computational homogenization: 2-6x speedup and 4-7x memory reduction versus existing TT-based approach.

### [Benchmarking Evidence Sufficiency in Vision-Language Models for Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32964v1)
**Authors:** Nhat Ha Nguyen, Sudipta Chowdhury, Lokesh Das
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** DriveAnswer benchmark tests whether driving VLMs can judge if visual evidence suffices to answer, not just answer correctly or localize actors.

**What's new**
- Introduces DriveAnswer: 20,151 questions across seven reasoning families with matched strong/weak-evidence pairs.
- Weak-evidence pairs preserve scene facts and answers but require abstention, separating sufficiency from correctness.
- Best zero-shot baseline hits 67.31% strong-evidence accuracy but only 21.55% on joint answer-abstain plus grounding.
- Controlled adaptation lifts Gemma-4-12B to 63.29% joint capability, nearly tripling zero-shot performance.
- Qwen3-VL-8B shows similar gains, supporting joint evaluation of correctness, sufficiency, and grounding.

### [Attestation is All You Need: Toward a Zero-Trust Architecture for HPC AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32958v1)
**Authors:** Jie Li, Chenxu Niu, Tongyang Wang, Yong Chen
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** AEGIS proposes behavioral attestation as a zero-trust runtime defense against hijacked authorized AI agents in HPC environments.

**What's new**
- Targets the hijacked authorized agent threat, which existing HPC cluster defenses do not address.
- Compiles task-derived behavioral constraints and monitors agent actions via eBPF syscall interception.
- Verifies evidence against constraint profiles and correlates behavior across multiple agents.
- Triggers Slurm-level containment when violations are detected.
- Detects all four implemented attack classes with sub-second median latency and no observed false positives.

### [LoomKV: Lossless KV Cache Compression from GPU Memory to SSD for LLM Serving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32954v1)
**Authors:** Wentao Zhu, Ziao Sheng, Peng Deng, Huiqi Hu, Xuan Zhou, Xing Wei, Zhuang Wang
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** LoomKV applies lossless KV cache compression to both external storage tiers and the GPU pool, boosting capacity and goodput for LLM serving.

**What's new**
- Calibrated per-head two-level 3-bit code over BF16 high bytes stores KV at 0.71 of raw size across nine models.
- Tier mode uses TileStream exact-length records and decompresses fetched records directly into scattered GPU pages.
- Pool mode uses FragPack to pack variable-length records into shared-budget GPU pages read by a fused decode-attention kernel.
- Tier mode delivers 2.7-3.6x raw goodput and 1.9-3.1x lower TTFT across LMCache's LRU cliff on AgentX.
- Pool mode fits 1.31-1.34x more GPU tokens and raises goodput 1.59x on a constrained AI IDE trace.

### [Do Vision-Language Models Know Their Own Visual Weaknesses? Probing Capability-Level Metacognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32940v1)
**Authors:** Dohoon Kim, Taesup Moon
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** VLMs largely fail to predict their own visual weaknesses in advance, though answer probabilities during solving reveal model-specific weaknesses.

**What's new**
- Probes capability-level metacognition across many open and proprietary VLMs, separating shared task difficulty from model-specific weakness.
- Finds VLMs misjudge overall accuracy, shared hard task types, and image degradation effects.
- Training VLMs to predict accuracy generalizes to unseen task kinds' accuracy levels but not to which kinds are hard.
- Model-specific weakness signals emerge in answer probabilities once the model begins solving a few unlabeled questions.

### [InstructReplay: Replaying Assistant Turns to Preserve General Behavior During Factual Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32918v1)
**Authors:** Xiaofeng Shi, Qian Kou, Hua Zhou
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** InstructReplay preserves general abilities during factual fine-tuning by replaying the model's own answers to general instructions, cutting benchmark decline from 28.1 to 2.4 points.

**What's new**
- Standard factual fine-tuning drops instruction-following, reasoning, and coding benchmarks by ~35 points on average.
- Losses track a collapse in answer length: fine-tuned models at least halve response length, and greater drift predicts more failures.
- InstructReplay keeps the model close to its initial answers on general instructions during fine-tuning.
- Reduces average decline on five general benchmarks from 28.1 to 2.4 points across three model families and two domains.
- Beats seven adaptation methods in all six settings; gain attributed to replaying own answers, not the loss form.

### [MXFP4 for LLM Inference: Complementary Post-Training Quantization Techniques and Their Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32873v1)
**Authors:** Natalia Frumkin, Alireza Khodamoradi, Kristof Denolf, Eric Dellinger, Stuart Biles
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** Study of MXFP6, MXFP4, and NVFP4 quantization shows effective LLM recipes are format-dependent, introduces Midmax scale selection, and finds MXFP6 activations with MXFP4 weights recovers most 4-bit accuracy loss.

**What's new**
- Effective quantization recipes are format dependent: MXFP4 benefits from rotation and error-correcting PTQ, NVFP4 less so.
- Introduces Midmax, a calibration-free scale-selection strategy that mitigates clipping in power-of-two block scales.
- MXFP6 activations with MXFP4 weights recovers most 4-bit accuracy loss; matches or beats BF16 baseline on Llama-3.1-8B.
- Adding a per-tensor macroscale to MXFP4 does not improve average accuracy, suggesting global rescaling alone is insufficient.

### [Path-probability gradients for optimizing force fields against dynamical observables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32851v1)
**Authors:** Zhongyao Wang, Lun Song, Yakun Zeng, Xingao Gong, Rui Shi, Mao Su
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** PathGrad optimizes machine-learning force fields against dynamical observables by differentiating Langevin path probabilities, yielding stable gradients for chaotic trajectories.

**What's new**
- Differentiates Langevin path probabilities rather than trajectories, avoiding unstable gradients from chaotic molecular dynamics.
- Gradient estimator accumulates on the fly from thermal noise and force derivatives, with variance stable as trajectory length grows.
- Validated against finite-difference calculations and used to fine-tune molten copper and water potentials against experimental diffusion coefficients.
- Joint optimization of water diffusion and O-O radial distribution also improved shear viscosity and rotational relaxation, which were not in the objective.
- Optimized potentials showed smaller force errors versus a higher-level quantum-chemical reference, indicating improved potential energy surface fidelity.

### [Measurement Artifacts in Trustworthiness Evaluations of Quantized Small Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32848v1)
**Authors:** Tanzeel Abbas, Tehseen Zia, David Windridge
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** Quantization trustworthiness evaluations conflate instrument artifacts with true effects; batch dependence and scoring choices distort measured changes in small language models.

**What's new**
- Interaction between measurement error and quantization can create or reverse apparent effects, not just shrink them.
- 4-bit harmful compliance rise is only half to two-thirds of the non-refusal rise from a corrected keyword detector.
- Cloze and lettered multiple-choice scoring correlate at rho=0.10 versus a ceiling of ~0.77, disagreeing beyond noise.
- LLM.int8() answers depend on batch composition and padding; rounding activations to 8 bits suffices to cause this; NF4 is batch-invariant.
- The two largest reported calibration losses are response-bias shifts, not genuine calibration changes.

### [Lattice Thermal Conductivity of MgO and the MgSiO$_3$ Polymorphs Bridgmanite and Post-Perovskite at Lower-Mantle Conditions Using Fine-Tuned MACE Potentials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32761v1)
**Authors:** Amrendra Kumar Tiwari, Sandro Jahn
**Published:** 2026-11-24
**Summary:** Fine-tuned MACE potentials replace DFT forces in phonon LBTE calculations, yielding lower-mantle thermal conductivities for MgO, bridgmanite, and post-perovskite.

**What's new**
- Phase-specific fine-tuned MACE potentials reproduce DFT energies, forces, stresses, and phonons for MgO, Pv, and PPv.
- Replacing MACE with DFT forces changes MgO lattice conductivity by only 0.2% at 300 K and 1.9% at 2000 K.
