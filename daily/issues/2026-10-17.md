### [LIBERO-PeRM: Benchmarking Personalized Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22372v1)
**Authors:** Zhixu Li, Keqian Tang, Litian Gong, Jingyu Yao, Wenqian Zhang, Tianze Xu, Zehao Wang, Yuping Wang, Junge Zhang, Jiachen Li
**Published:** 2026-10-15
**Summary:** LIBERO-PeRM is a new benchmark for personalized robotic manipulation that separates task completion from preference satisfaction across five research questions.

**What's new**
- Introduces LIBERO-PeRM, a controlled benchmark for preference learning in embodied agents.
- Defines a taxonomy of language-expressible preferences covering motion, skill, and user-profile choices.
- Provides a procedural pipeline generating paired demonstrations with and without preferences.
- Creates five task suites with 312 tasks, high-quality demonstrations, metadata, and preference metrics.
- Shows VLA policies struggle with zero-shot personalization; SFT makes preferences learnable but generalization and conflict handling remain hard.

### [Discovering state-of-the-art quantum circuits with large language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22367v1)
**Authors:** Andi Gu, Alek H. Kemeny, Susanne F. Yelin, Mikhail D. Lukin
**Published:** 2026-10-15
**Summary:** An evolutionary framework where LLMs propose quantum circuits and an evaluator scores them discovers circuits beating state-of-the-art Toffoli and qubit counts in seven of eight tasks.

**What's new**
- LLM-proposed circuits verified and scored by an independent evaluator in an evolutionary loop
- Seven of eight tasks show significant reductions in both Toffoli and qubit counts
- Eighth task yields a new reversible architecture for prime-field modular inversion
- New architecture makes classical divsteps viable for reversible integer arithmetic
- Opens path to evaluator-guided LLM co-design of quantum algorithmic structures

### [T2-z: Test-Time Optimization of Environment Latents for Robotic World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22364v1)
**Authors:** Chenyuan Zhou, Yining Hong, Huang Huang, Yunfan Jiang, Wenlong Huang, David Held, Vitor Campagnolo Guizilini, Yue Wang, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu, Leonidas Guibas, Yejin Choi
**Published:** 2026-10-15
**Summary:** T2-Z optimizes environment latents at test time to adapt robotic world models to unseen environmental factors, cutting prediction error and boosting action success.

**What's new**
- Introduces T2-Z for test-time optimization of environment latents in robotic world models.
- Alternates training between world-model optimization and environment-latent optimization.
- At test time, optimizes a new latent from interactions while keeping the world model frozen.
- Reduces object prediction error from 15.20 to 7.00 across 7 simulated and 4 real-world tasks.
- Raises action success from 33.73% to 72.96% and infers latents from a single trajectory.

### [Rethinking Routing Exploration in Modern Mixture-of-Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22353v1)
**Authors:** Bingcong Li, Hao Ma, Michael Muehlebach, Niao He, Yi Ma
**Published:** 2026-10-15
**Summary:** MirrorMoE resolves an exploration dilemma in modern MoE designs by token-adaptively suppressing unnecessary experts to shrink the routing space.

**What's new**
- Identifies an exploration dilemma between fine-grained expert segmentation and shared experts in MoE models.
- Shared experts reduce token representation variability, lowering routing exploration.
- Fine-grained segmentation enlarges the routing space combinatorially, demanding more exploration.
- Proposes MirrorMoE, which shrinks the exploration space via token-adaptive expert suppression.
- MirrorMoE consistently improves performance across model scales.
