## Featured Papers

### [AgentTime: Can Agents Estimate and Control Their Own Runtime?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09944v1)
**Authors:** Michael Ofengenden, Maksym Andriushchenko
**Published:** 2026-10-07
**Summary:** AgentTime benchmarks whether AI agents can follow requested durations, forecast their runtime, and estimate elapsed time, finding current agents poor at all three.

**What's new**
- Introduces AgentTime, 222 tasks from 18 sources covering coding, computer use, agentic work, and automated research.
- Duration-following: Fable 5.1 deviates 2.9x from requested runtimes; GPT-6 Astra only 1.2x.
- Matching runtime doesn't mean working: 14 of 158 classifiable Astra runs explicitly slept after finishing.
- Forecasting experiments show agents tend to overestimate their natural runtimes.
- Removing temporal info more than doubles elapsed-time deviation for Sol and Astra, nearly doubles for Fable.

### [Many Ways to Succeed: Diversity-Driven RL Fine-Tuning for VLA Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09943v1)
**Authors:** Haoru Li, Jinmei Liu, Zhiyong Wang, Xiaoming Li, Zhenhong Sun, Daoyi Dong, Chunlin Chen, Zhi Wang
**Published:** 2026-10-07
**Summary:** DRIVE introduces a diversity-driven RL fine-tuning objective that rewards successful-behavior diversity, improving VLA generalization to out-of-domain tasks and real-world deployment.

**What's new**
- RL fine-tuning selectively reshapes exploration: contracts behavior globally but diversifies successful trajectories and covers more latent task-valid solutions.
- DRIVE groups rollouts under matched task conditions and derives a success-conditioned intrinsic reward from relative behavioral diversity.
- DRIVE avoids rewarding diverse failures or superficial timing differences by using temporal alignment and success conditioning.
- Improves average OOD performance by 5.3 points on π0 and 2.0 points on π0.5 across LIBERO-Plus, ManiSkill3, and RoboTwin 2.0.
- On a dual-arm AgileX PiPER-X platform, DRIVE increases average OOD success from 64.1% to 73.3% (+9.2 points).

### [Fast holographic inversion of superconducting domes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09938v1)
**Authors:** Sejin Kim
**Published:** 2026-10-07
**Summary:** A new training method speeds up holographic inversion of superconducting domes from days to minutes, revealing which parts of the scalar mass function a dome can determine.

**What's new**
- Inversion time cut from days to 10-60 minutes via gradient from two horizon/boundary integrations.
- Derivative of critical temperature condition with respect to M needs no extra integration.
- Dome constrains M only where horizons reach; interval between lowest doping and Fsq=0 is undetermined.
- Pinned-mass reconstructions agree where horizons reach, including minima of M.
- For YBCO and 2M-WS2, pinned mass rests on ties, leaving scalar mass open.

### [RollVerify: Bridging Efficiency and Accuracy in Long-Tail Rollout Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09914v1)

**Published:** 2026-10-07
**Summary:** RollVerify is a lightweight RL framework that verifies and repairs partial-rollout samples via an off-policy shift metric to match on-policy accuracy while cutting training cost.

**What's new**

- Actively verifies and repairs samples before training instead of passively reweighting them.

### [Nonuniform Screening Reshapes Collective Excitons in Molecular Aggregates from Stochastic Bethe-Salpeter Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09924v1)
**Authors:** Barry Y. Li, Chern Chuang, Tim Duong, Tucker Allen, Justin R. Caram, Daniel Neuhauser
**Published:** 2026-10-07
**Summary:** Stochastic Bethe-Salpeter theory shows nonuniform screening reshapes exciton dispersion and bright-dark ordering in large cyanine dye aggregates.

**What's new**
- Ab initio stochastic Bethe-Salpeter Equation (sBSE) applied to planar cyanine aggregates with up to 2,856 valence electrons.
- Transferable parameterized screened exchange kernel unifies dielectric response, exciton delocalization, and optical spectra.
- sBSE transition densities validate the real-space Frenkel exciton picture and yield first-principles screened couplings.
- Calculations reproduce H-, I-, and J-like spectral evolution in molecular aggregates.
- Spatially dependent screening reshapes exciton dispersion and changes relative energies of bright and dark states.

### [Defensive Sufficiency in a Stackelberg Model of AI Security](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09892v1)
**Authors:** Subhabrata Majumdar, Rajlakshmi Chavan
**Published:** 2026-10-07
**Summary:** Study of when feedback-driven AI defenses provide sufficient protection and when investing in proactive discovery and reactive repair is economically worthwhile.

**What's new**
- Shows attack surface is defended with probability 1 if every unresolved attack has persistent discovery chance, repairs are effective, and updates preserve protection.
- Derives completion-time bounds and extends to growing attack surfaces, generalizing repairs, and multiple discovery mechanisms.
- Distinguishes eventual protection against each fixed attack from complete protection at a single time.
- Formulates defender-led Stackelberg game with proactive discovery and reactive repair, characterizing least-cost deterrence and equilibrium regimes.
- Numerical experiments show faster repair reduces compromise duration without reducing compromise probability.
