### [Lab earthquakes confirm the theory of frictional slip pulses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26065v1)
**Authors:** Alina Shafir, Tom Gabrieli, Yuval Tal, Eran Bouchbinder
**Published:** 2026-08-26
**Summary:** Lab experiments confirm the theory of frictional slip pulses, showing they are intrinsically slow, unsteady rupture modes with a predicted decay-to-growth transition.

**What's new**
- First extensive experimental validation of the 2D theory of frictional slip pulses.
- Confirms pulses are categorically unstable, evolving slowly over time.
- Demonstrates the predicted transition from decaying to growing pulses in the lab.
- Tests dynamics across varying prestress, nucleation conditions, and fault roughness.

### [Observation of thermal Hall effect in diamond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25726v1)
**Authors:** Marcin Matusiak, Andrzej Ptok, Maria Szlawska, Kamran Behnia
**Published:** 2026-08-26
**Summary:** First observation of a large thermal Hall effect in diamond, suggesting an intrinsic phonon contribution.

**What's new**
- Measured thermal Hall conductivity in diamond, peaking near the longitudinal thermal conductivity maximum.
- Observed the largest thermal Hall signal ever recorded, 340 W/(m K) at 10 T.
- The ratio kappa_xy/kappa_xx follows the trend seen in other insulators.
- Proposes that the effect is intrinsic to phonon gas, with amplitude explained by fundamental constants.
