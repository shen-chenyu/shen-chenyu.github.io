### [ConceptGuard: Benchmarking Context-Sensitive Unlearning in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20338v1)
**Authors:** Sahil Kale, Ian Harris
**Published:** 2026-08-20
**Summary:** ConceptGuard benchmarks context-sensitive unlearning in LLMs using dual-use concepts, revealing current methods' poor contextual separation and trade-offs.

**What's new**
- Introduces dual-use concepts for evaluating unlearning in harmful and benign contexts.
- Constructs ConceptGuard benchmark with complementary forget and retain sets.
- Shows current unlearning techniques perform poorly on intent-sensitive evaluation.
- Reveals strong forgetting-utility trade-offs and limited contextual sensitivity gains.

### [AI4AI-Bench: Benchmarking LLM Agents in Algorithmic Design for Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20318v1)
**Authors:** Yizhe Chi, Wenyi Li, Deyao Hong, Xiaoqiu Wang, Mingju Gao, Kaisen Yang, Bingxiang He, Youjie Zheng, Calvin Xiao, Qinhuai Na
**Published:** 2026-08-20
**Summary:** AI4AI-Bench benchmarks LLM agents on rewriting training algorithms for recursive self-improvement, showing current agents barely improve on existing baselines.

**What's new**
- Introduces 10 frozen research repositories spanning 10 training algorithm families for agent evaluation.
- Agents have 4 hours to rewrite training algorithms, then code is rerun and scored against original.
- Across 29 configurations, mean score is 0.166; best system reaches 0.250, closing under a fifth of the gap to optimum.
- Most submissions never change how the model learns; those that do score 0.226 vs 0.126 for others.
- More reasoning effort increases the fraction of such submissions from 8% to 64% and mean score from 0.094 to 0.196.

### [Phantom Gains: Auditing Self-Improvement Against a Measured Null](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20290v1)
