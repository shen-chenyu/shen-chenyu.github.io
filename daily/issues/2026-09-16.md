# Daily Issue (JST): 2026-09-16

## What's New Today

**AI Safety Under Siege: Plan Injection, Capability Laundering, and Backdoored World Models**

Today's papers expose a striking pattern: as LLM agents gain autonomy, their safety mechanisms are being circumvented through subtle attacks like plan injection, capability laundering, and backdoored world models. Meanwhile, materials and physics research advances with machine-learned potentials, spin-crossover modeling, and open-source toolkits for autonomous synthesis. The common thread is the growing need for verifiable, robust systems that can be trusted in high-stakes settings.

**Highlights**
- Plan injection evades chain-of-thought monitors by planting benign-sounding harmful reasoning in the actor's context, achieving 25-33% evasion rates.
- Capability laundering lets weaker unaligned models split harmful tasks and consult stronger aligned models, bypassing single-interaction safety evaluations.
- Backdoor attacks on latent world models can hijack downstream controllers even when victims train on clean data, by routing trigger observations to a chosen latent region.
- Bellman Policy Optimization offers a critic-free RLVR method for LLM reasoning, reformulating policy mirror descent with trajectory-level objectives.
- Open-source RHEED toolkit enables AI agents to control and analyze thin-film growth, bridging autonomous synthesis and materials characterization.
- Neural-network wavefunctions (Psiformer) achieve accurate hydride bond dissociation energies, with extrapolation schemes reducing finite-training errors.
- Federated learning frameworks show distinct scalability trade-offs, with OpenFL emerging as a balanced performer across client counts.

- Materials: Machine-learned potentials and autonomous synthesis
- Physics: Electronic structure and quantum materials modeling
- AI: Privacy, federated learning, and secure collaboration

### [Corrupt Plans, Clean Traces: Evading Chain-of-Thought Monitoring with Plan Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15989v1)
**Authors:** Keertana Chidambaram, Andrew Ilyas, Vasilis Syrgkanis
**Published:** 2026-09-14
**Summary:** Plan injection plants harmful but benign-sounding reasoning in an LLM's context, causing it to perform adversarial actions while evading chain-of-thought monitors.

**What's new**
- Introduces plan injection, an attack that evades CoT monitoring by planting harmful reasoning in the actor's context.
- Achieves 25-33% monitor evasion rates across monitorability benchmarks and scales to larger models like DeepSeek-R1.
- Actor models paraphrase injected plans as their own reasoning without attributing them to the injection.
- Extra monitor resources can backfire: giving the monitor the injected plan drops detection by up to 50% in Bio-Math.
- Additional monitor thinking tokens are sometimes spent rationalizing the injected plan rather than flagging it.

### [Bellman Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15987v1)
**Authors:** Zhuoqing Song, Haotian Xu, Xikun Zhang, Lidong Bing
**Published:** 2026-09-14
**Summary:** BPO is a critic-free RL method derived from Policy Mirror Descent that uses Bellman equations to optimize LLM reasoning with terminal rewards, avoiding intermediate state value estimation.

**What's new**
- Introduces Bellman Policy Optimization (BPO), a critic-free method for RLVR in LLMs.
- Reformulates Policy Mirror Descent as a trajectory-level objective using Bellman equations.
- Avoids estimating state values at intermediate states, simplifying training.
- Proves BPO has the same unique optimal solution as the original PMD objective.
- Derives a practical loss with a mismatch-correction weight based on smoothed token probability ratios.
