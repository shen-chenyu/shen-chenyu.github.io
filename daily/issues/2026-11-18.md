### [TRAIL-IRL: Crossed Reward-Temperature Mixtures for Unlabeled Trajectory Clustering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22715v1)
**Authors:** Yichen Zhu, Guanhua Fang, Ming Zheng
**Published:** 2026-11-17
**Summary:** TRAIL-IRL clusters unlabeled trajectories by jointly modeling reward preferences and action randomness via crossed reward-temperature mixtures, with identifiability guarantees.

**What's new**
- Introduces crossed mixture pairing each reward with shared temperature levels, letting action randomness vary within preference groups.
- Establishes identifiability conditions for rewards, temperatures, mixing weights, and class counts from long trajectories.
- Proves common-temperature mixtures cannot reproduce crossed mixtures with multiple temperatures under same constraints.
- Develops generalized EM with BIC class-count selection; simulations show better fit, reward recovery, and classification.
- White stork data: BIC favors four reward classes and three temperatures; age links to migration vs. retention classes.

### [Know When to Zoom: How Small Is Too Small for Vision-Language Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22709v1)
**Authors:** Siddharth Vohra, Min Xu
**Published:** 2026-11-17
**Summary:** Study finds online VLMs lose small-image detail (readable text grows from ≤6px to 10-13px on 7680x4320 photos), and tiling or two-step zoom recovers much of it.

**What's new**
- Measured detail loss across seven models from four vendors using a printed-code eye exam and 601 small-object questions.
- Splitting photos into up to nine tiles raises Claude and Gemini accuracy by 11.1-14.5 points; GPT-6.1 Sol jumps 50.7% to 83.7%.
- Two-step zoom (model names region first) matches or beats tiling on Gemini and nears it on Claude using ~40% of image tokens.
- When detail is lost, Gemini gives confident wrong answers 43-48% of the time versus Claude's 5-9%.
- Tiling only after an unsure Claude reply comes within 1.4 points of always tiling at 62-70% of image tokens; five zoom rules and code released.

### [Remembered as Brief: Stored Preferences SilentlyRemove Decision-Critical Information](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22697v1)
**Authors:** Joshua Adrian Cahyono
**Published:** 2026-11-17
**Summary:** AI assistant memory of user preferences can silently drop decision-critical warnings, and models then make value-dependent choices for users.

**What's new**
- Introduces a 100-decision, 25-domain benchmark varying only the assistant's memory of the user.
- Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite and DeepSeek V4 Pro stored a planted 'no warnings' preference in every episode.
- 93% and 84% of their later answers missed a critical item, almost always silently.
- Same models chose options for users on 80% and 95% of value-dependent choices vs. none with neutral memory.
- Listing must-know points before applying the user profile restored content in four of five models.

### [Quadrature-Free Radial Intracule Densities: A Bessel-Harmonic Formulation for Gaussian Basis Sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22683v1)
**Authors:** Evelio Francisco, José Antonio Quiñonero, Ángel Martín Pendás
**Published:** 2026-11-17
**Summary:** Exact quadrature-free radial intracule densities via Bessel-harmonic expansion of Hermite Gaussian products, with binary128 fixing cancellation errors.

**What's new**
- Removes angular quadrature entirely for Cartesian Gaussian bases and arbitrary second-order density matrices.
- Expands coupling exponential in modified spherical Bessel functions and real spherical harmonics, terminating at l=p_x+p_y+p_z.
- Atomic limit reduces to finite sum of C_i R^{n_i} e^{-mu R^2} terms; radial moments given via confluent hypergeometric 1F1.
- Identifies catastrophic cancellations in angular-momentum coupling and removes them by elevating to binary128.
- Recovers sum rules to 7-8 digits from LiH to Kr2, versus up to 22% error for Euler-Maclaurin and Lebedev quadratures.

### [Domain walls of a spin-chirality ferroelectric as tunable quantum particles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22662v1)
**Authors:** Aleksandr Buzdakov, Atharva Hingane, Sergey Artyukhin
**Published:** 2026-11-17
**Summary:** DMRG shows a chirality domain wall in a gapped frustrated spin-1/2 XXZ chain behaves as a tunable quantum particle with box-like levels and field-driven motion.

**What's new**
- Domain wall of spin chirality acts as a quantum particle in a finite chain, with particle-in-a-box energy levels and wave functions.
- Under an electric field the wall moves like a particle under constant force.
- In the infinite chain its mass grows over two orders of magnitude as bond alternation δ decreases.
- Mass diverges at δ*≈0.012 where the band bottom flattens; below δ* the band has two degenerate off-center minima.
- Large mass arises from near cancellation of in-plane and out-of-plane exchange energies.

**What's new**
- Characterizes the minimum excess prediction-set size needed for hard coverage guarantees over T rounds.
