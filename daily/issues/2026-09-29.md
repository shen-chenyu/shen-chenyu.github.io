### [The Decomposition Tax: LLM Pipelines Lose Up to 40 Accuracy Points at Their Own Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32825v1)
**Authors:** Tianqi Bu, YuXuan Peng, Junteng Tu, Henghui Xiao
**Published:** 2026-09-26
**Summary:** LLM pipelines lose up to 40.5 accuracy points at stage interfaces; re-grounding after the lossy interface and preserving relationships in quantity lists reduce the tax.

**What's new**
- Decomposition tax: up to 40.5 accuracy points lost at interfaces in a four-stage LLM pipeline.
- 70 of 118 primary-family tests survive Benjamini-Hochberg correction; 54 survive Holm.
- Rewriting one stage's instruction moves gemma-3-12B's tax from 4.5 to 36.5 points.
- Adding 'every relationship stated between them' to a quantity-listing stage lowers tax on 9 of 9 models.
- Re-grounding after the lossy interface beats re-grounding before it on 7 of 7 models on both benchmarks.

### [Sliced Orlicz-Wasserstein](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32847v1)
**Authors:** Binh Thuan Tran, Khai Nguyen
**Published:** 2026-09-26
**Summary:** Sliced Orlicz-Wasserstein (SOW) generalizes sliced Wasserstein by replacing L^p with Luxemburg norm, offering flexibility and efficiency.

**What's new**
- SOW is a metric on measures with finite Orlicz norm and recovers SW when φ(x)=x^p.
- Convergence under SOW implies weak convergence; converse holds under compact support.
- Sample complexity for SOW and its powered functional is minimax optimal.
- Monte-Carlo and bisection algorithms approximate SOW with error and complexity analysis.
- Experiments show SOW is more efficient than OW and more flexible than SW in two-sample tests and generative models.
