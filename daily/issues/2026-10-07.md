# Daily Issue (JST): 2026-10-07

## What's New Today

**AI Agents Under Pressure: Memory, Safety, and Coordination Gaps**

Today's papers probe the limits of LLM agents across memory, safety, and coordination, revealing that correct records and successful experiences can still hide failures. Meanwhile, materials and physics work advances simulation and discovery with machine-learned potentials and quantum hardware. A recurring theme: evaluation and verification lag behind capability.

**Highlights**
- Agent memory systems can be contaminated by speculation or re-reads, causing stale or false facts to persist.
- Tool-using agents often fail to establish evidence before acting, and corrupted tool feedback can drop accuracy from 100% to 72%.
- Multi-agent coordination improves with learned collaboration priors and self-referenced social preferences, but zero-shot settings remain challenging.
- Machine-learned interatomic potentials and self-learning agents accelerate materials discovery, from alloy nanoparticles to X-ray diffraction.
- Quantum processors demonstrate parallel fragment-based quantum chemistry, while variational quantum circuits tackle compositional generalization.
- Safety alignment faces new threats: answer-side backdoors and skill poisoning via successful experiences.

**Themes**
- AI: Agent memory and reliability
- AI: Multi-agent coordination and evaluation
- AI: Safety, security, and alignment
- Materials: Machine-learned potentials and simulation
- Physics: Quantum computing and spin dynamics
- AI: Efficient inference and adaptation

### [Partially Observable Zero-shot coordination by Predicting Intention of Partner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08142v1)

**Summary:** PIP predicts partner intention via a Joint-view VAE and partner-state belief networks to enable zero-shot coordination when the partner is intermittently out of view.

**What's new**
