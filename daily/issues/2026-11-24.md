# Daily Issue (JST): 2026-11-24

## What's New Today

**AI agents, materials simulation, and the reliability of learned models**

Today's papers span agentic systems, LLM fine-tuning, and machine-learned materials simulation. A common thread is verification: whether agents can be trusted, whether benchmarks measure what they claim, and whether learned potentials and world models support real decisions. Several studies also probe the limits of distillation, routing, and memory in deployed models.

- PseuDRAGON reuses expert judgments to stabilize financial-data pseudonymization with local LLMs.

- VOPD couples verifiable rewards with on-policy distillation for denser, grounded supervision.
- Temporal leakage audit finds TGB node-affinity leaderboard evaluations are not point-in-time correct.
- RoboAda corrects frozen robot policies at test time for unseen physical changes.

## Featured Papers

### [PseuDRAGON: Knowledge-Guided Local LLMs for Financial-Data Pseudonymization in Closed Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30353v1)
**Authors:** Gwangui Hong, Seonho An, Seonkyu Lim, KyungTae Lim, Min-Soo Kim
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** PseuDRAGON uses knowledge-guided local LLMs with externalized judgments and learned rules to pseudonymize financial data consistently in closed networks.

**What's new**
- Keeps confirmed judgments and corrections outside the model, reusing them via exact-name lookup and learned rules.
- Expert review workflow searches institutional policy, then national privacy law, then international sources.
- Rules raised label reuse from 75 to 83 under identical approval checks with no reuse errors.
- Withholding non-PII rules for unseen names removed extra PII misses at 25% training judgments.
- History-first baseline cut automated identification time by 83.0% on average across three paired runs.

### [AutoClip-DPZO: Practical Private Clipping Selection for Differentially Private Zeroth-Order LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30352v1)
**Authors:** Xinghe Chen, Haichao Sha, Zihao Wang, Xiaokui Xiao, XiaoFeng Wang, Wei Dong
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** AutoClip-DPZO selects the DPZO clipping threshold via private quantile estimation, avoiding costly fine-tuning search while matching optimal-C utility.

**What's new**
- Reduces clipping threshold selection in DPZO to a private quantile estimation problem, eliminating repeated fine-tuning-based search.
- Derives a novel characterization of DPZO error versus C capturing both noise variance and clipping bias.
- Target quantile depends only on public privacy and training parameters, so no sensitive hyperparameters are needed.
- Achieves utility comparable to the optimal C with negligible additional overhead and accounted-for privacy cost.

### [RepoClean: Evaluating Coding Agents on Repository-Level Obsolete Code Cleanup](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30350v1)
**Authors:** Hongyixuan Yuan, Qixing Zhou, Pengcheng Huang, Qingshuo Ni, Runsong Zhao, Shaokai He, Xiangpeng Li, Deqiang Ouyang
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** RepoClean benchmarks LLM coding agents on removing obsolete code from Python repositories; best model GPT-6 Astra solves only 23% of 200 tasks.

**What's new**
- Introduces RepoClean, a 200-task benchmark from 34 open-source Python repos for repository-level obsolete code cleanup.
- Tasks built by reintroducing real deleted code into newer versions, keeping cases where prior tests then fail.
- Agents must use cross-file dependencies and repo interfaces to decide what to remove versus preserve.
- Best model, GPT-6 Astra, resolves only 23% of tasks, showing cleanup remains hard.
- Agents remove far less obsolete code when cleanup is not explicitly requested.

### [Strain-Induced Tetragonal-to-Cubic Transition in MAPbI$_3$ from Machine-Learned Molecular Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30348v1)
**Authors:** Daan Holleman, Luuk Klein Nagelvoort, Monica Morales-Masis, Linn Leppert
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** Machine-learned MD at 300 K predicts a tetragonal-to-cubic transition in MAPbI3 at ~0.5% tensile biaxial strain, with band gaps blue-shifting under tension unlike static DFT.

**What's new**
- Uses on-the-fly machine-learned force field MD to capture finite-temperature strain response of MAPbI3
- Predicts tetragonal-to-cubic transition at ~0.5% tensile strain, matching epitaxial KCl experiments
- MA cations keep preferential orientations in the strain-induced cubic phase, unlike the thermal cubic phase
- MD ensemble band gaps blue-shift under tensile strain, while static averaged-structure DFT predicts a red shift
- Surface effects decay within a few octahedral layers, giving consistent bulk and slab trends

### [High-Throughput Screening of Phononic Bandgap in Experimentally Realized Materials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30344v1)
**Authors:** Mohammad Bagheri, Kameyab Raza Abidi, Pekka Koskinen
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** Universal ML interatomic potentials screen 42,347 stable compounds for phonon band gaps, finding 11,176 gap materials and low-thermal-conductivity candidates.

**What's new**
- Screened 154,879 materials and ran phonon calculations on 42,347 thermodynamically stable, experimentally observed compounds.
- Identified 11,176 materials with phononic gaps: 10,960 optical-optical and 270 acoustic-optical.
- Analyzed lattice thermal conductivity to find acoustic-optical gap candidates with low conductivity.
- Confirmed low room-temperature lattice thermal conductivity in three Li-metal binaries via first-principles Boltzmann transport.

### [Atomistic insight into defect-mediated boron redistribution and high-temperature strengthening in TiAl alloys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30330v1)
**Authors:** Xiang Meng, Shuhei Shinzato, Zhiqiang Zhao, Linfeng Yu, Jian Guo, Liangli Chu, Kepeng Ouyang, Shigenobu Ogata, Zhuhua Zhang
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** Machine-learning-potential simulations reveal how boron redistributes among defects in TiAl alloys and strengthens them at high temperature by pinning dislocations and suppressing twinning.

**What's new**
- Hybrid GCMC/MD with a new high-fidelity machine-learning potential models dilute B in TiAl.
- Interstitial B is trapped at Ti-rich octahedral sites but kinetically converts to substitutional dopants near Al vacancies.
- B enriches at semi-coherent gamma/alpha2 boundaries and preferentially partitions into the alpha2 phase.
- 1 at.% B raises average flow stress by 9.4% at 900 K while reducing shear strain accumulation.
- B impedes dislocation glide via local pinning and suppresses deformation twinning.

### [Verifiable On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30325v1)
**Authors:** Juntong Shi, Stefano Ermon, Jure Leskovec, Minkai Xu
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** VOPD transfers sparse verifier rewards into dense, verifier-grounded teacher supervision for on-policy distillation, improving reasoning performance and learning efficiency.

**What's new**
- Projects verifier preference onto the teacher's implicit-reward direction to ground dense supervision in verified outcomes.
- Rao-Blackwellizes the signal over the full vocabulary, extending verifier supervision to unsampled actions.
- Objective interpretable as adaptively extrapolating the distillation target based on teacher-verifier agreement.
- Improves final performance and learning efficiency over vanilla OPD across two teacher-student setups and eight math benchmarks.

### [SPECTRA-RCWA: A Spectral Propagation Engine for Coupled-Wave Theory with Recursive Admittance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30305v1)
**Authors:** Hyoseok Park
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** SPECTRA-RCWA is an RCWA forward/adjoint solver that avoids layer-matrix diagonalisation using entire-function matrix products and admittance recursion, enabling fast, stable gradients.

**What's new**
- Replaces per-layer eigendecomposition with three entire functions of the half-size layer matrix via matrix products.
- Uses admittance recursion or scattering-matrix doubling to keep evanescent orders stable in thick layers.
- One adjoint field yields gradients for all design values without a computational graph; solves batch together.
- 144 solves with 578x578 matrices take 0.25 s on one GPU, 218x faster than differentiable eigendecomposition.
- Maintains single-precision accuracy in layers several wavelengths thick and is 47-65x faster than TorchRDIT at matched accuracy.

### [Temporal Leakage Distorts the TGB Node Affinity Leaderboard: A Point-in-Time Audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30289v1)
**Authors:** Yuhang Wang, Dongmei Li, Yuze Sun, Xiaolong Qu, Yanxiang Wang, Lei Yao
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** Audit finds all four TGB node affinity datasets violate point-in-time correctness, causing temporal leakage that inflates NDCG@10 by up to 17% and reshuffles the leaderboard.

**What's new**
- Formalizes point-in-time correctness by separating a label's query time from its availability time, using as-of joins and bitemporal databases.
- All four TGB node affinity datasets and their evaluation loop fail the audit: three feed labels back before their documented window, one processes source edges before prediction.
- Under a leakage-free protocol, leaking methods lose up to 17% NDCG@10 and the edge of past labels over the interaction stream nearly vanishes or reverses.
- Best configurations and leaderboard rankings change; a training-free long-memory statistics blend matches or beats the state of the art under both protocols.
- Authors urge benchmarks to build in point-in-time correctness and note their tools transfer to other window-aggregated label benchmarks.

### [Learning Heterogeneous Reasoning in Large Language Models: From Mode Compatibility to Local Bottlenecks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30281v1)
**Authors:** Sunbowen Li, Dandan Song, Shengkun Ma, Xuanning Cao
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** Mixed SFT learns heterogeneous reasoning modes without labels; scaling shifts the bottleneck from global mode confusion to local state-tracking errors, only a few of which are outcome-determining.

**What's new**

### [Attributing Security Outcomes in LLM-Agent Evaluations: The Role of Content-Free State](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30259v1)
**Authors:** Abdelmonaim Bounite, Zangir Iklassov
**Published:** 2026-11-23
**Summary:** LLM-agent security verdicts depend on content-free state like hash seeds and tool order, so ASR comparisons need attribution testing before being trusted.

**What's new**
- Introduces TARE, a framework that attributes reported effects only if they exceed measured influence of content-free state.
- On ASB, fixed hash seeds give identical runs, yet changing the seed alters up to 17% of verdicts while ASR moves at most 5 points.

- TARE attributes effects to two of three defenses, one in opposite directions, and confirms a 4.8-point decrease from the third.

**Summary:** Auditing SWE-Gym shows many unsolved coding tasks are mislabelled by false-negative tests; repairing them yields better RL training data and higher held-out scores.

**What's new**
- 74% of tasks a 9B base model never solves have tests that reject correct fixes (false negatives), vs 20% of sometimes-solved tasks.
