### [On the Diffusibility of High-Dimensional Latents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28473v1)
**Authors:** Chao Feng, Zhiyang Xu, Bowei Chen, Yuanjun Xiong, Xiyao Wang, Jui-Hsien Wang, Richard Zhang, Zhe Lin, Andrew Owens, Yijun Li
**Published:** 2026-09-23
**Summary:** Finetuning encoders for reconstruction shrinks latent dimensionality, making x0-prediction better than velocity prediction for flow matching in high-dimensional feature spaces.

**What's new**
- Finetuning pretrained encoders for faithful reconstruction recovers fine details but reduces effective representation dimensionality.
- Standard velocity prediction in flow matching must fit orthogonal noise directions outside the low-dimensional signal manifold.
- This makes optimization inefficient in high-dimensional latent spaces.
- Clean data parameterization (x0-prediction) focuses learning on the underlying signal manifold instead.
- Across multiple strong-reconstruction encoders, x0-prediction consistently improves text-to-image generation.

### [StudentBench: AI and human tutoring yield equivalent GRE learning gains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28470v1)
**Authors:** Curtis Northcutt, Inaara Hasmani, Kevin Feng, Trevor Khangi, Andreas Plesner, Jonas Mueller
**Published:** 2026-09-23
**Summary:** StudentBench study finds AI tutoring statistically equivalent to expert human tutoring for GRE learning gains, with one AI tutor matching humans at 918x lower cost.

**What's new**
- AI tutoring matched expert human tutoring for GRE learning gains (p = .015) across 2,383 participants.
- Best AI tutor surpassed the human tutor in five of seven GRE domains on average.
- One AI tutor achieved equivalent gains at 918x lower cost: $0.0052 vs $4.81 per percentage point.
- 2,028 pairwise rubric evaluations by expert tutors compared LLM lesson plans and practice problems.
- Faster AI replies correlated with more student messages, more correct practice, and larger gains (p < .002).
