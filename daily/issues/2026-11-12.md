# Daily Issue (JST): 2026-11-12

## What's New Today

**Foundation models, agent memory, and physics-aware ML dominate today's papers**

Today's batch spans medical foundation-model adaptation, agent memory and self-evolution, and a strong showing of physics- and chemistry-informed machine learning. Several papers also probe the reliability of benchmarks and evaluation, from cross-validation variance to judge auditing.

**Highlights**
- DINOv2 adaptation is compared against task-specific U-Nets for medical segmentation, with held-out-site testing.
- AutoScaffold and Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution both address when and how LLM agents should learn from experience.
- TabPFN outperforms tuned baselines on energetic-material property prediction without hyperparameter tuning.
- New theory shows REINFORCE-style policy optimization can suffer coverage collapse, motivating coverage-preserving algorithms.
- Pyrite benchmarks protein-ligand pose ranking, where expert chemists perform near chance.
- Cross-validation variance gets an exact finite-sample identity, clarifying how splits affect estimates.

### [Medical Image Segmentation: Foundation Model Adaptation versus Task-Specific Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14519v1)
**Authors:** Emre Kutlusan, Yongmin Li
**Published:** 2026-11-10
**Summary:** Adapted DINOv2 generalised better than from-scratch U-Nets on every external medical dataset, though task-specific models stayed competitive in-distribution.

**What's new**
- Foundation model beat task-specific U-Nets/ResU-Nets on all external datasets across colonoscopy, breast ultrasound and dermoscopy.

- Only a small fraction of DINOv2 needed adaptation; required capacity was set by imaging domain, not adaptation method.
- A widely used polyp test set was found to duplicate images from another benchmark.

**What's new**

### [A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14467v1)
**Authors:** Audrey Huang, Nan Jiang, Sham M. Kakade, Sadhika Malladi, Akshay Krishnamurthy, Dylan J. Foster
**Published:** 2026-11-10
**Summary:** Theory shows REINFORCE-style post-training can collapse base-model response coverage, and that resetting the KL reference to the latest policy restores coverage-preserving convergence.

**What's new**
- Identifies coverage collapse: REINFORCE, basis of PPO/GRPO, can drive optimal-response probability near zero even as reward improves.
- Shows convergence can need exponentially many steps despite good initial coverage, even with exact gradients and KL regularization.
- Proposes reference resets, formalized in Approximate Policy Mirror Descent (APMD), to mitigate coverage collapse.
- Gives first global convergence guarantees that PPO/GRPO variants with KL and resets, plus squared-loss methods like Kimi's, are coverage-preserving.

### [A Theory of Cross-Validation Variance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14463v1)
**Authors:** Nathaniel S. O'Connell, Jaime L. Speiser
**Published:** 2026-11-10
**Summary:** Exact finite-sample variance identity for cross-validation shows K-fold is first-order variance optimal and leave-one-out minimizes variance among K-fold schemes.

**What's new**
- Proves an exact finite-sample identity for cross-validation variance via observation weights in training and evaluation roles.
- Decomposes variance into sampling variance (training size only) and fold-splitting variance from weight changes across draws.
- Shows K-fold is first-order variance optimal; Monte Carlo and bootstrap CV are not.
- First-order K-fold variance is independent of K; leave-one-out has smallest variance under reasonable conditions.
- Establishes asymptotic normality of the CV estimate with the identity's full variance, enabling inference.

### [Quantifying the Sampling Frequency Invariance of Time-Series Foundation Models for Sensor Data Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14425v1)
**Authors:** Sourish Gunesh Dhekane, Abinaya Kumar, Torsten Woertwein, Yan-Bo Lin, Thomas Ploetz, Shabnam Ghaffarzadegan
**Published:** 2026-11-10
**Summary:** First study to stress-test four time-series foundation models for sampling-frequency invariance, introducing three metrics that tie degradation to information bandwidth loss rather than raw frequency.

**What's new**
- Introduces FSI, FDI, and CDI metrics to quantify embedding shift, forecasting, and downstream degradation under sampling frequency change.
- Stress-tests MOMENT, Chronos-2, Moirai-MoE, and TimesFM across three dimensions of sampling frequency variation.
- Shows invariance depends on the margin between native sampling frequency and task-required bandwidth, not the frequency value itself.
- Separates how much degradation stems from information loss versus model limitations, and how much model choice explains invariance.
- Reframes sampling-frequency invariance as a measurable, signal-aware property of the signal-model pair.
