## What's New Today

**AI Systems Under Scrutiny: Benchmarks, Memory, and Physical Limits**

Today's papers push AI evaluation beyond leaderboard scores, auditing implementation gaps, agent trust, and memory lifecycle management. In parallel, materials and physics work advances quantum geometry, crystal structure prediction, and self-assembly with machine-learned potentials. Across both domains, the theme is reliability: what models actually know, what they forget, and how to verify it.

- IdeaAMBIG benchmarks 660 research-idea specifications for implementation-critical gaps, exposing where methods are underspecified for faithful reproduction.

- A positive resolution of the gap-entropy conjecture pins down optimal sample complexity for best-arm identification.

### [IdeaAMBIG: Benchmarking Implementation-Critical Gaps in Research-Idea Specifications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10539v1)
**Authors:** Yiling Ma, Yilun Zhao, Sihong Wu, Manasi Patwardhan, Arman Cohan
**Published:** 2026-09-09
**Summary:** IdeaAMBIG benchmarks whether research-idea specifications are codification-ready, finding defect localization is the main bottleneck for LLMs.

**What's new**
- Introduces IdeaAMBIG, a 660-instance benchmark for implementation-critical gaps in research-idea specifications.
- Includes 163 real-world gaps from reproducibility reports and GitHub issues plus 497 controlled synthetic gaps.
- Evaluates codification-readiness assessment, defect localization, and clarification action generation.
- Best of 13 LLMs reaches only 9.6% Macro Defect Recovery Rate on real-world instances.
- Given the defect, clarification success hits 80.6%; gold resolutions raise codification-ready rate from 14% to 98%.

### [A positive resolution of the gap-entropy conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10529v1)
**Authors:** P. M. Aronow, Nathan Kallus, Patrick Lopatto
**Published:** 2026-09-09
**Summary:** Proves the gap-entropy conjecture for fixed-confidence best-arm identification with Gaussian arms, showing optimal sample complexity is Θ(H(log(1/δ)+Ent(I))).

**What's new**
- Proves the gap-entropy conjecture for fixed-confidence best-arm identification with Gaussian arms.
- Optimal expected samples averaged over arm permutations is within constant factors of H(log(1/δ)+Ent(I)).
- Provides an instance-independent algorithm achieving this bound plus a g^{-2} log log(e^e/g) term.
- Resolves a long-standing open problem in the theory of best-arm identification.

### [Fortunate Recall: Ontology-Driven Memory Lifecycle Management for Persistent Coherence in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10413v1)
**Authors:** Ansuman Mullick, Eray Tüzün
**Published:** 2026-09-09
**Summary:** Fortunate Recall is an ontology-driven memory lifecycle policy layer that improves LLM memory coherence by classifying facts and applying category-specific decay, supersession, and retrieval.

**What's new**
- Introduces a 10+1 behavioral ontology for personal facts with category-specific lifecycle policies.
- FR-Bank achieves 76.9% on LifecycleBench and 75.2% on LongMemEval-S, outperforming Mem0, A-MEM, Memory-R1, and MemoryOS.
- Ablation shows generic lifecycle metadata drives correctness, while the ontology halves confabulation (12.0% vs 24.2%).
- End-to-end, FR-Bank cuts confabulation from 45.1% to 22.4% over answered queries and from 32.2% to 13.0% over all queries.
- Results replicate on Kimi K2.5 and transfer to BEAM, with ontology benefits concentrated in contradiction resolution.

### [Constrained Optimization of Higher-Order Cluster-Expansion Hamiltonians for Alloys Using Simulated Bifurcation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10310v1)

**Published:** 2026-09-09
**Summary:** Simulated bifurcation directly optimizes higher-order cluster-expansion Hamiltonians for alloys, avoiding quadratic reformulation and recovering known ordering trends.

**What's new**
- Formulates cubic and quartic cluster-expansion Hamiltonians as penalty-augmented PUBO problems for binary alloys.
- Uses SQBM+ simulated-bifurcation solver to directly handle higher-order polynomial objectives without auxiliary variables.

### [Training Trajectories Determine Circuit Removability in Annealable Soft-Prior Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10287v1)

**Published:** 2026-09-09
**Summary:** Whether soft-prior Transformer circuits survive prior removal depends on the training trajectory, not just the final architecture.

**What's new**
- Unforced models collapse at zero gate (0.095) despite strong performance with prior active (0.772).
- Smooth fade-to-zero training preserves high zero-gate accuracy (0.734).
- Forced-zero training, hard switching, and post hoc continuation fail to recover the effect.
- Pattern replicates on Markov induction; linear regression ICL is a boundary case.
- Circuit consolidation occurs after the gate reaches zero, with varying responsible heads across seeds.
