### [PolyGPT: A Decoder-Only Transformer for Polymer Electrolyte Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07438v1)
**Authors:** Tanmay Sarkar Akash, Siddhartha Das
**Published:** 2026-11-05
**Summary:** PolyGPT, a 38.3M-parameter decoder-only Transformer, generates polymer repeat units from property targets and chemical constraints, but electrolyte conditioning sharply reduces output diversity and constraint satisfaction.

**What's new**
- Introduces PolyGPT, a 38.3M-parameter decoder-only Transformer for conditional polymer electrolyte generation.
- Unconditional and electrolyte-only generation reach 99.46% and 99.59% structural validity, respectively.
- Electrolyte conditioning drops uniqueness among valid outputs from 99.99% to 51.74%.
- Electrolyte conditioning cuts distinct valid polymers within 50 K of Tg target from 82.35% to 32.31%.
- Feature-inclusion success falls from 94.27% to 31.27% under domain conditioning, showing property error alone is insufficient.

### [Projection-based segregated, iterative, and multi-stage physics-informed neural network for incompressible Navier-stokes equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07387v1)
**Authors:** Zhenyu Zhang, Zhixiang Ling, Yinghao Huo, Huayong Liu, Yunde Su
**Published:** 2026-11-05
**Summary:** SIM-PINN decouples incompressible Navier-Stokes into momentum and pressure Poisson equations with projection-based iterations and multi-stage networks, improving convergence and accuracy.

**What's new**
- Segregated, iterative, multi-stage PINN inspired by the classical projection method in CFD.
- Decouples nonlinear NS equations into linearized momentum and pressure Poisson equations solved separately.
- Dedicated multi-stage networks couple via projection-based inner iterations, refining velocity at targeted frequencies.
- Alleviates optimization difficulties from nonlinearity, constraint coupling, and spectral biases.
- Demonstrated on steady lid-driven cavity and unsteady Kolmogorov flows.

### [Two-stage residual learning for calibrated uncertainty in solar irradiance forecasts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07444v1)
**Authors:** RR Flanagan, E Kasha, PW Talbot, AS Epiny, MR Deinert
**Published:** 2026-11-05
**Summary:** A two-stage LSTM method models forecast error separately from expected irradiance, yielding better-calibrated uncertainty in solar forecasts.

**What's new**
- Two-stage approach: first LSTM predicts mean irradiance, second models forecast error distribution.
- Explicit error modeling cuts predicted-vs-historical std deviation by 34.6% for standalone LSTM.
- Coupling LSTM with Gaussian process regression reduces that deviation by 56.5%, the best configuration.
- Captures total predictive uncertainty rather than only the model contribution estimated by dropout methods.
- Validated on National Solar Radiation Database weather data using probability integral transform evaluation.

### [Changepoint localization via nested exchangeability tests: from point estimates to confidence intervals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07399v1)
**Authors:** Rohan Hore, Aaditya Ramdas
**Published:** 2026-11-05
**Summary:** CLONE builds distribution-free confidence intervals around any changepoint estimate by testing exchangeability in nested windows, needing only O(log n) permutation tests.

**What's new**
- Introduces CLONE, a nested exchangeability testing method for distribution-free changepoint localization intervals.
- Provides finite-sample coverage for any black-box reference estimator under exchangeable pre/post segments.
- Uses a geometrically shrinking radius grid, requiring only O(log n) permutation tests.
- Characterizes optimal scores and gives practical recommendations yielding sharp intervals.
- Shows asymptotically vanishing normalized interval length, supported by simulations and image/text experiments.

### [Data-Driven Modeling of Molten Chloride Corrosion Using Static Test Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07125v1)
**Authors:** Md Mahmudul Hasan Pathik
**Published:** 2026-11-04
**Summary:** A machine-learning framework trained on 291 literature static tests predicts molten chloride corrosion rates with cross-validated R² of 0.85 and MAE of 0.41 mg cm⁻² day⁻¹.

**What's new**
- Compiled 291 literature static molten chloride corrosion experiments into a structured dataset for ML modeling.
- Introduced a Chloride Salt Preparation Index (CSPI) to encode salt treatment, atmosphere, and impurity condition numerically.
- Blended tree-based ensemble achieved cross-validated R² of 0.85 and MAE of 0.41 mg cm⁻² day⁻¹.

- SHAP and sensitivity analyses flagged exposure time, temperature, and alloy chemistry as key predictors; conformal intervals were well calibrated.

### [Cross-Family Mode Coupling and Localization Reversal in Phononic Crystal Defect Dimers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07422v1)
**Authors:** Xinlin Xu, Junji Kato
**Published:** 2026-11-05
**Summary:** Defect dimers in phononic crystals show localization reversal driven by cross-family modal exchange between B1 singlet and E doublet, not just frequency shifts.

**What's new**
- Localization reversal occurs when softening one defect shifts a continued modal branch toward its unchanged partner.
- Off-axis and diagonal layouts show reversal with family exchange; opposing B1 and E contributions nearly cancel at threshold.
- Deleting cross-family stiffness and mass blocks removes reversal, identifying modal redistribution as the dominant mechanism.
- Nominal operators retaining full singlet-doublet space predict thresholds within 1.1% without fitting detuned trajectories.
- Frequency agreement alone does not guarantee localization accuracy; independent channels can predict the opposite defect.

**Published:** 2026-11-05
**Summary:** Reweighting can fake fairness gains: it improves FNR parity partly by worsening false negatives for the higher-prevalence group, especially when that group is a minority.

**What's new**
- Simulations across 3,000 datasets (7-14% prevalence) show reweighting can mislead fairness assessments.
- FNR for the higher-prevalence minority group rose ~6 points (~82 missed cases per 10,000).

- Pattern held across six classifiers from logistic regression to neural networks.
- Overall accuracy stayed essentially unchanged, hiding the shift from aggregate metrics.
