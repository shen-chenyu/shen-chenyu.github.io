### [ParVL: Parallel Scaling and Expandable Compute Allocation for Multimodal LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04010v1)
**Authors:** Yang Yang, Qinyu Zhao, Mouxiang Chen, Xiaohui Li, Lixin Gu, Wenhai Wang, Hongjie Zhang, Wenwei Zhang
**Published:** 2026-08-04
**Summary:** ParVL introduces a parallel scaling framework for MLLMs that reuses backbone parameters across vision and language branches, enabling flexible compute allocation and improved performance.

**What's new**
- Parallel scaling by reusing ViT and LLM backbones across multiple branches.
- Systematic study of compute allocation trade-off between vision and language.
- End-to-end fine-tuning on ~13B tokens with branch-specific prefixes.
- Outperforms single-branch baselines; optimal allocation varies by task.

### [WorldCup Arena: Prospective, Leakage-Free Evaluation of Frontier LLMs on a Live Tournament](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04008v1)
**Authors:** Zhenran Wang, Zhonghan Bian, Jinsong Li, Zhangyang Qi
**Published:** 2026-08-04
**Summary:** A prospective, leakage-free benchmark evaluating six frontier LLMs on 4,494 World Cup match predictions, revealing shared behaviors and limited differentiation.

**What's new**
- First live-tournament LLM forecasting benchmark with no retrospective answers.
- All 104 matches scored across 7 markets plus group winners and outright pool.
- LLMs match bookmaker favorites but under-commit to draws and goals.
- Accuracy drops in close matches despite richer dossiers; majority vote adds nothing.
- Systems show narrow margins and churn in middle standings, not sharply differentiated.

### [Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04001v1)
**Authors:** Mohsen Hariri, Weicong Chen, Nahal Shahini, Vikash Singh, Kai Ye, Amirhossein Samandar, Debargha Ganguly, Sreehari Sankar, Yanyan Zhang, Shouren Wang, Jerry Peng, Biyao Zhang, Michael Hinczewski, Vipin Chaudhary
**Published:** 2026-08-04
**Summary:** A systematic framework for test-time scaling in reasoning LLMs, distinguishing inference regimes, evaluation principles, and reproducibility requirements.

**What's new**
- Formalizes test-time scaling as budgeted inference over an implicit prefix tree, defining three structural regimes.
- Introduces an evaluation profile that separates system performance from candidate-bank diagnostics.
- Prescribes protocol-matched reporting of compute and uncertainty for fair comparisons.
- Specifies reproducibility requirements, distinguishing exact replay from distributional reproducibility.
- Releases over 2 billion reasoning traces with verifier and token-level signals.

### [Understanding and Designing Phase Change Materials: Insights from Atom Probe Tomography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03932v1)
**Authors:** Jan Köttgen, Nils von den Driesch, Alexander Pawlis, Matthias Wuttig
**Published:** 2026-08-04
**Summary:** This paper reviews how atom probe tomography reveals metavalent bonding in crystalline phase change materials, explaining their unique properties.

**What's new**
- Introduces Probability of Multiple Events (PME) as a bonding descriptor from APT.
- Shows crystalline PCMs have PME >55%, indicating metavalent bonding.
- Amorphous PCMs behave as covalent solids with lower PME.
- Maps metavalent solids in a quantum chemical bonding map.
- Uses bonding map to explain property trends for PCM applications.
